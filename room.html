Why another songwriting membership

Most of them sell you a library. I want to give you a room.

I've looked closely at the songwriting memberships out there. The good ones have great teachers and hundreds of hours of lessons. Almost all of them run on one time zone, treat cowriting as a message board, and count your progress in posts and likes.

Lessons don't finish songs. Rooms do. People you write with every week, notes that tell you the truth, and a calendar built for Manila, Jakarta and Sydney, not just one time zone on the other side of the world.

So this room is built around doing the work together, on a schedule, with the money side explained from day one. And unlike the others, there's a real house at the end of it.

The Pairing · finding people to write with

Every week, we put you in a group of three to write a song together.

Writing with other people is the fastest way to get better, but finding the right people is hard. So every Tuesday the room matches you with two other writers whose skills fit with yours. You write one song together that week. If you can't make a week, just sit it out.

  1. 1

    Say what you're good at

    Words, melody, playing an instrument, or making beats and tracks. Add the languages you write in, the styles you like and where you live. You only do this once.

  2. 2

    Say "I'm in" for the week

    By Monday night, tick a box if you have time to write that week. Busy? Leave it unticked and you won't be matched.

  3. 3

    Meet your group on Tuesday

    You're put with two writers whose skills fill your gaps and who are awake at the same time as you. You also get a song idea to start from, in case you need one.

  4. 4

    Agree who owns what, then write

    Before you start, all three of you sign a short form that says how the song is shared: 30% each, and 10% to the house. Then you write it, and post it in the room for feedback.

Example · what Tuesday's message might look like

WordsWriter in Quezon City · writes in Taglish
MelodyWriter in Jakarta · English and Bahasa
Music and beatProducer in Melbourne · English

When you'll meet: Thursday, 7–9pm Manila time, online.

Song idea to start from: a goodbye at an airport, told by the person who stays behind.

Ownership form attached: 30% each, 10% to the house. You keep all of your writer's share.

How every song is shared

Every song written together in the Room, or at a camp, is shared the same way. You keep all of your writer's share, the royalties paid straight to songwriters. The publishing is split 90% between the writers and 10% to the house.

The house's 10% pays for the work it does on every song: registering it with FILSCAP or your home society, keeping the paperwork straight, and pitching the best songs to artists, brands and sync. The house only earns when your song earns. Songs you write on your own stay 100% yours.

Why sign first? Because "we'll sort it out later" is how friendships and songs fall apart.

Notes that help

Kind and honest, both. In a format you can use.

"Love it!" doesn't help, and neither does a teardown. Every note in the room uses the same shape, so it's quick to give and easy to act on.

  • Give two, get one. Reply to two songs for each song you post. That keeps the Feedback Room fair.
  • The Pro Desk. Once a month, put one song in front of a mentor from the house for written notes. Want a full video review? See Song Reviews.
  • The live Round. Play it, hear the room react, and get notes in real time.

Example note · "Jeepney Window"

Works

The second verse names the exact stop. I was on that jeepney.

Try

The chorus arrives at 1:10. Cut the second half of verse one and get there by 0:45.

Question

Who is waiting at the end of the route? The song wants to tell us.

Circles · by application · opening 2027

Small rooms for the market you want to write for.

Once you're finishing songs, apply to a Circle: up to twelve writers and a mentor who works in that market. You get a real brief each month, notes from someone who buys songs, and a pitch when a song is ready. Nobody can promise a placement. What a Circle promises is that the right person hears your best work.

The house rules, short version

Five things I promise, and ask of you.

1

One fair split, up front. You keep all of your writer's share. Publishing is 90% to the writers and 10% to the house, on every song written together.

2

Splits on the day. Every cowrite ends with a signed split sheet, even for a rough idea.

3

Ask before you share. Nobody posts, pitches or releases anyone else's work without written permission.

4

No selling in the feed. No playlist schemes, no "promotion" offers. Wins are for your releases.

5

A safe room. No harassment, hate or pressure. The hosts act on every report.

Questions

About the room

Where does the room actually live?

In a private community app on your phone (iOS and Android) and in your browser. Prompts, pairings and notes arrive as notifications, so the room fits around your day.

I'll never get to Manila. Is it still for me?

Yes. Everything on this page works from anywhere. The house is there for when you're ready, not a condition of joining.

I'm just starting out. Will I be out of my depth?

No. The prompt and the Feedback Room are built for early writers, and everyone starts the 5 Keys on the bottom line. Circles are for later.

What if I miss the live sessions?

Every House Session and Round is recorded and posted in the room. The prompt runs all week, and you opt in to the Pairing only when you can write.

Can I write in Filipino, or another language?

Yes. Write in whatever language the song wants. The Pairing takes your languages into account when it puts groups together.

Who owns what I write in a Pairing?

You and your cowriters: 30% each, with 10% to the house, written on the form you all sign before you start. You keep all of your writer's share.

Why does the house get 10%?

Because it does real work on every song: registering it with FILSCAP or your home society, keeping the split sheets, and pitching the best songs to artists, brands and sync. The house only earns when the song earns, so it has every reason to get your songs heard. Songs you write on your own stay 100% yours.
