Member agreement

Who owns the songs you write here.

The song ownership agreement every member accepts, in plain English. It covers songs written together in the Room and at camps.

Last updated: October 2026 (draft)

Draft for review by a Philippine lawyer before launch. This text explains how Gising Sining House intends to work. It isn't final, and nothing here is binding until it has been reviewed and published as final.

What it covers

Songs you write together with other members in the Room (the Pairing, open times, writing rooms) or at a Gising Sining House camp. It doesn't cover songs you write on your own, or songs you wrote before you joined.

Your writer's share stays yours

Every song has a writer's share and a publishing share. You keep 100% of your writer's share: the royalties your collecting society (FILSCAP or your home society) pays straight to songwriters. The house never takes any of it.

The publishing share: 90% writers, 10% house

The publishing share is split 90% between the writers, equally unless you all agree something different in writing before you start, and 10% to Gising Sining House.

Writers in the roomEach writerGising Sining House
Two45%10%
Three (a Pairing group)30%10%
Four (a camp room with a producer)22.5%10%

What the house does for its 10%

  • Keeps the signed split sheet and the song's details.
  • Registers the song with FILSCAP, and helps each writer register with their home society.
  • Pitches the best songs to artists, labels, sync and brands, and tells the writers about every pitch.
  • Collects the publishing income it administers and sends each writer their share, with a statement at least once a year.

The house only earns when a song earns.

Solo songs and songs you bring in

Songs you write on your own stay 100% yours, even if you post them in the Room for notes. Songs you wrote before joining stay yours too.

Signing

Every cowrite has a split sheet that each writer signs before you start, in your house tools or on paper. Joining the house means you agree to this arrangement for songs written together; the split sheet records the names and shares for each song.

Writers aged 16 or 17 sign with a parent or guardian, who co-signs this agreement and every split sheet. The song and its share still belong to the young writer.

If you leave

Songs already written keep the split everyone signed, whether or not you stay a member. New songs you write after leaving aren't covered.

If there's a disagreement

The signed split sheet decides. If something is unclear, talk to your cowriters first, then ask the host to help. If that doesn't settle it, Philippine law applies.
