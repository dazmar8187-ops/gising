How to run your first cowrite

Your first cowrite will probably feel awkward. Everyone's does. Here's how to make it work anyway.

Darryl Saunders · 10 October 2026

Cowriting with strangers sounds terrifying. In practice, most of the fear is in the first ten minutes. After that you're just people trying to make a song better. Here's a shape that works, whether you're in the Pairing, at a camp, or in someone's living room.

Before you start

  • Bring two or three unfinished ideas: a title, a line, a melody on a voice memo. You may not use them, but you won't walk in empty.
  • Know what you bring. Lyric, melody, chords, production. Say it early, so the room knows how to use you.
  • Agree the split first. Two minutes, before anyone writes. In the house, that's the ownership form in the writing room.
  • Agree the time. Two to three hours is plenty. A deadline helps songs finish.

The first half hour

Don't write yet. Talk. What's everyone listening to? What's happened this week? The best songs often come out of a story someone tells before the session "starts".

Then pick one idea and commit. A strong title is the fastest start: it tells you what the chorus has to say.

While you write

  • Say yes before you say no. Try someone's idea for a minute before judging it. "What if…" beats "That doesn't work."
  • Write the chorus first if you can. It's the song's promise; the verses just have to earn it.
  • Record everything. Voice memo every melody, even the bad ones. You'll forget the good one otherwise.
  • Keep one shared lyric sheet everyone can see.

When it stalls

It will. Take ten minutes, change rooms, sing the chorus out loud, or work on a different section. If a line won't come, write a placeholder and keep moving. Finishing a rough song beats polishing half of one.

Finishing

  • Play the whole song top to bottom, even if parts are rough.
  • Record a simple work tape: one voice, one instrument.
  • Decide who does what next: demo, lyric fixes, registration.
  • Post it for notes. In the Room, that's the Feedback Room, with every writer's name.

After

Message your cowriters the next day. If it worked, book another session. The writers you click with are worth more than any single song.
